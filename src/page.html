Birch in, glucose syrup out

A physics and chemistry model of the POC v2 flowsheet: milled birch and water, a 400 °C / 25 MPa supercritical-water reactor, flash quench, a water-only finishing hold, filtration and two nanofiltration steps. Set the glucose you want per day; the model finds the wood, the tube sizes and what comes out with it.

    What the model assumes

    Solid ground

    • Water density, enthalpy, viscosity and conductivity come from IAPWS-95 on a 22.5–30 MPa, 10–700 °C grid; saturation data for both flashes.
    • Energy balances at the mixing tee, reactor wall, both flashes and the steam preheater. Mass and carbon close independently.
    • Pressure effect on ionic reactions through the Marshall–Franck ion product of water.
    • Activation energies for cellulose (154.4 and 430.3 kJ/mol), oligomer hydrolysis (135.2) and glucose isomerisation (111.5) from Cantero et al., J. Supercrit. Fluids 75 (2013).

    Calibrated, re-fit on Stage A data

    • Rate constants at 400 °C are set so pure cellulose gives over 95% sugars at 0.02 s and about 60–70% glycolaldehyde at 1 s, and biomass gives about 65% C5+C6 sugars at 0.19 s.
    • In-particle diffusivity is one fitted number. It makes yield fall with the square of particle size, as the pilot work reports.
    • The published anchors are wheat bran and beet pulp. No birch data exist in this model.

    Weakest parts

    • The finishing hold. Acid-catalysed oligomer hydrolysis is inferred from analytical post-hydrolysis practice, and the pH from the wood’s own acids is an estimate.
    • Membrane rejections and steam volatilities are assumed constants. The plan’s G3 and G4 gates exist to measure them.
    • Plug flow with no axial dispersion, no fouling, no char, no gas formation, no corrosion products.
    • Product safety is not modelled. Glycolaldehyde, furans and phenolics in the syrup are reported, not judged.
